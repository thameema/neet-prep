## Objective A: Which individual NEET questions were disputed in 2024, the June 2024 re-exam, 2025 and 2026, and how was each resolved?

### Takeaway
Only three disputes have a documented final resolution: the 2024 "atoms" Physics question (two options accepted, then cut back to option 4 alone by the Supreme Court on IIT Delhi advice), the Re-NEET 2026 Vernier calliper question (dropped, +4 to everyone), and the court challenges of 2025 and 2026 (all dismissed, keys unchanged). Of the leads supplied, Golgi apparatus and surface tension were not found anywhere, and rolling motion and Vernier were not found as 2024 disputes; the Vernier dispute is real but belongs to the 2026 re-exam.

### Cited Findings

#### A1. NEET 2024 (5 May 2024): the "atoms" Physics question

- Question text (opened): "Given below are two statements: Statement I: Atoms are electrically neutral as they contain equal number of positive and negative charges. Statement II: Atoms of each element are stable and emit their characteristic spectrum. In the light of the above statements, choose the most appropriate answer from the options given below." Options: (1) Statement I is incorrect but Statement II is correct; (2) Both are correct; (3) Both are incorrect; (4) Statement I is correct but Statement II is incorrect. — [ThePrint](https://theprint.in/india/row-over-neet-ug-physics-question-iit-d-experts-say-there-is-only-one-correct-answer/2186709)
- NTA's key was option 4, per the same article. — [ThePrint](https://theprint.in/india/row-over-neet-ug-physics-question-iit-d-experts-say-there-is-only-one-correct-answer/2186709)
- NTA received 13,373 challenges to the provisional key for this one question. Its subject experts then "held that two options be taken as correct in place of one option" because old and new NCERT editions differ. — [Careers360](https://news.careers360.com/neet-ug-results-2024-physics-problem-toppers-reduce-61-17-nta-mcc-counselling-revised-merit-list-latest-update-link); [The South First](https://thesouthfirst.com/health/neet-2024-goof-up-controversy-surrounding-rank-inflation-due-to-bonus-marks-for-faulty-physics-question/)
- NTA's stated reason was that older books used "each" and newer books used "most". A parent quoted in the article disputes this: he says the 2022 NCERT reads "atoms of most elements are stable and emit characteristic spectra" and that his daughter's 2019 NCERT has the same wording. — [The South First](https://thesouthfirst.com/health/neet-2024-goof-up-controversy-surrounding-rank-inflation-due-to-bonus-marks-for-faulty-physics-question/)
- Of 67 candidates at 720/720 on 4 June 2024, NTA said 17 had no grace marks, 44 were "on account of the revision in one answer key of Physics", and 6 were due to compensatory marks for lost time. — [The South First](https://thesouthfirst.com/health/neet-2024-goof-up-controversy-surrounding-rank-inflation-due-to-bonus-marks-for-faulty-physics-question/)
- Resolution: a three-member IIT Delhi physics panel, constituted by the IIT Delhi director on the Supreme Court's request, concluded only option 4 is correct. A Delhi University associate professor consulted separately said the same. — [ThePrint](https://theprint.in/india/row-over-neet-ug-physics-question-iit-d-experts-say-there-is-only-one-correct-answer/2186709)
- The Supreme Court accepted the report ("option 4 represents the only correct answer to the question"), refused to cancel the exam on 23 July 2024, and ordered a revised merit list. — [Careers360](https://news.careers360.com/neet-ug-results-2024-physics-problem-toppers-reduce-61-17-nta-mcc-counselling-revised-merit-list-latest-update-link)
- Topper counts are three separate figures: 67 (original, 4 June 2024), 61 (after compensatory marks were withdrawn), and 17 (Careers360's own projection after the physics revision, assuming all 44 had chosen the option later rejected; not an official count). The article itself is inconsistent, saying "44 of 61" in the lede and "44 of 67" in the body. — [Careers360](https://news.careers360.com/neet-ug-results-2024-physics-problem-toppers-reduce-61-17-nta-mcc-counselling-revised-merit-list-latest-update-link)
- Lawyers told the court there were three groups: those who picked the option later deemed incorrect (minus five relative to peers), those who picked the option held correct (four marks), and those who skipped. — [ThePrint](https://theprint.in/india/row-over-neet-ug-physics-question-iit-d-experts-say-there-is-only-one-correct-answer/2186709)
- Lead tag: CONFIRMED, with one correction. The Supreme Court did not "decide between" two keys so much as reverse NTA's two-option concession and restore the original single key.
- Conflict to carry forward: a search snippet of a Business Standard explainer said NTA's key "named option 1" (snippet only; the page returned HTTP 403). ThePrint says option 4. Option numbering may differ by booklet code; unresolved.

#### A6. How the answer-key challenge process works

- 2025: provisional key 3 June, challenge window 3 to 5 June, final key 14 June; fee Rs 200 per question, described as non-refundable. — [PW](https://www.pw.live/neet/exams/neet-2025-answer-key) (SEO-grade)
- 2026 re-exam: provisional key 25 June, window closed 28 June; fee Rs 200 per question, with documentary evidence from NCERT or other standard references; this page says the fee is refunded if the challenge is accepted. — [K Square Institute](https://ksquareinstitute.in/blog/neet-2026-final-answer-key-changes/) (SEO-grade; conflicts with PW on refundability)
- Possible outcomes: correct option revised, multiple options accepted, question dropped (+4 to all), or no change. — [K Square Institute](https://ksquareinstitute.in/blog/neet-2026-final-answer-key-changes/)
- Challenges are reviewed by a panel of subject experts, and NTA's position in court is that the panel's finding stands. — [LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/amp/high-court/delhi-high-court/neet-ug-2026-re-exam-nta-answer-key-biology-question-545319)
- NCERT wording was the stated basis for NTA's 2024 two-option decision. — [The South First](https://thesouthfirst.com/health/neet-2024-goof-up-controversy-surrounding-rank-inflation-due-to-bonus-marks-for-faulty-physics-question/)

### Inferences
- Practical lesson, atoms 2024: where an older NCERT line and the current one differ, the current edition's wording and plain physics won in the end. A student should answer from the current rationalised NCERT and not rely on a remembered older line; a concession won at the challenge stage can be reversed later.
- Practical lesson, qualifier words: the 2024 dispute turned on "each" versus "most". This is a real examined instance of the qualifier-word trap discussed under Objective B.
- Practical lesson, Vernier 2026: when the worked answer matches no option, a drop with +4 for everyone is the likely outcome, so the rational move in the hall is to spend little time, mark the nearest option or leave it, and move on. The only documented drop in this period was a question with no correct option at all.
- Practical lesson, court route: every court challenge found for 2025 and 2026 failed, including one by a 695-scorer. The only effective remedy is the Rs 200 challenge inside the short window (two to three days), backed by an NCERT page reference.
- Practical lesson, "arguably also correct" Biology items (cardiac regulation 2025, Q150 2026): NTA held its key each time. Choose the option matching the most direct NCERT sentence, not the most complete physiological truth.
- The 7.6 versus 7.7 dispute appears to belong to the cancelled 3 May 2026 paper (the article predates the 12 May cancellation), so it was probably never resolved because the exam was voided. This is inferred from dates, not stated by any source.
- The "Vernier caliper" lead for 2024 is most likely a misattribution of the 2026 re-exam drop.
- The PW table's two question numbers per 2025 code is consistent with "two questions with two accepted options" in the final key; whether the provisional key already had one of them is unclear.

## Objective B: What exam-hall tactics and NCERT "hidden zone" practices do high scorers describe?

### Takeaway
The best-sourced first-person account (Re-NEET 2026 AIR 2, 715/720) is: Biology, then Chemistry, then Physics; skip anything not solved immediately and return in a second round; bubble the OMR as each answer is confirmed. Most other specifics in circulation (minute budgets, guessing thresholds, NCERT "hidden line" lists) come from coaching blogs, not from named toppers, and almost no source traces a real past question to a specific NCERT box, caption or summary.

### Cited Findings

Attempt order, time budget, rounds:
- Re-NEET 2026 AIR 2 (715; 701 in the cancelled May paper): "I started with Biology followed by Chemistry and Physics." "If I couldn't solve a question immediately, I skipped it during the first round." He retried in a second round and left still-doubtful ones to the end. He gave no per-subject minutes. — [Careers360](https://medicine.careers360.com/articles/re-neet-2026-air-2-pranshul-bansal-interview)
- Coaching guide for the 180-question, 180-minute paper: Biology 45 to 50 minutes including OMR, Chemistry 50 to 55, Physics about 60, leaving 15 to 20 minutes to revisit marked questions; no more than 2 minutes on a question in round one; a weak subject can go last. — [MyStudyCart](https://mystudycart.com/blog/neet-paper-attempting-strategy/) (coaching blog)
- A topper quoted on that page (AIIMS Delhi student) used three rounds: about 2 hours 20 minutes going in sequence from Q1 through roughly 150 familiar questions; about 20 minutes on those needing thought; then, for roughly 5 with no idea, eliminate two options and guess, or leave blank if elimination fails. — [MyStudyCart](https://mystudycart.com/blog/neet-paper-attempting-strategy/) (his account likely predates the 2025 pattern change; year not given)

OMR bubbling:
- AIR 2, 2026: "I filled the OMR simultaneously while solving the paper", bubbling each answer once confirmed, to cut bubbling errors. He found only one wrong response when the OMR was released. — [Careers360](https://medicine.careers360.com/articles/re-neet-2026-air-2-pranshul-bansal-interview)
- Coaching guide: bubble as each question is solved or after each section; never leave all bubbling to the end. — [MyStudyCart](https://mystudycart.com/blog/neet-paper-attempting-strategy/)
- Snippet only, not opened (Aakash page returned 404; Neetopedia and others not fetched): advice splits between per-question bubbling and batches of 5 to 10 or 10 to 15; a skipped question can misalign every later bubble, so match the question number each time.

Mid-exam recovery and composure:
- AIR 7, NEET 2025: "Many aspirants know the answers but lose marks because they panic." "The most important thing you should carry with you to the examination hall is confidence." — [Careers360](https://news.careers360.com/neet-2026-preparation-tips-by-air-7-keshav-mittal-revision-pyq-tricks-for-may-3-exam)
- AIR 23, 2026: "Never panic after a poor mock test." — [Careers360](https://medicine.careers360.com/articles/neet-2026-air-23-manasvi-kulshrestha-interview)
- Coaching guide: go easy, then moderate, then tough; keep rough work ordered so calculations can be rechecked. — [MyStudyCart](https://mystudycart.com/blog/neet-paper-attempting-strategy/)

Silly mistakes, error logs, mocks:
- AIR 23, 2026: "Maintain a mistake notebook, revise your errors regularly and ensure you never repeat the same mistakes." Mock scores in the final month were "consistently above 690", mostly over 700. — [Careers360](https://medicine.careers360.com/articles/neet-2026-air-23-manasvi-kulshrestha-interview)
- AIR 7, 2025: prioritise revisiting mistakes made in mock tests; "Even small corrections can lead to big improvements in rank." Review formula sheets, commonly forgotten points and data-based facts in the last 15 days. — [Careers360](https://news.careers360.com/neet-2026-preparation-tips-by-air-7-keshav-mittal-revision-pyq-tricks-for-may-3-exam)
- Coaching guide: take mocks between 2 and 5 pm to match the exam slot. — [MyStudyCart](https://mystudycart.com/blog/neet-paper-attempting-strategy/)

Trap types:
- Exception sentences introduced by "Except", "However", "But", "Unlike", "In contrast" are flagged as frequently tested; examples of exceptions listed: mature RBCs lack nucleus and mitochondria, CAM plants open stomata at night. — [Cerebrum Biology Academy](https://cerebrumbiologyacademy.com/blog/ncert-reading-strategy-neet-biology-2026)
- Statement-pair and assertion-reason formats were the vehicle for the most contested items in 2024 and 2025 (atoms, fig, tapetum). — [ThePrint](https://theprint.in/india/row-over-neet-ug-physics-question-iit-d-experts-say-there-is-only-one-correct-answer/2186709); [CollegeDekho](https://www.collegedekho.com/news/bonus-marks-in-neet-2025-list-of-controversial-questions-67170/)
- Snippet only, not opened (Edufever, Deeksha, Disha, Resonance blogs): absolutes ("always", "never", "all", "none") often turn an NCERT-looking statement false; EXCEPT / INCORRECT stems cause many silly errors, so underline the negative and read the last line first; attempt in round two only if about 70 percent sure, and guess only after eliminating two options; in Physics check unit consistency and closely spaced options.

### Inferences
- Biology first, Chemistry second, Physics last is the dominant order: one first-person 715 scorer and the coaching guides agree. No opened source reports a high scorer starting with Physics (a Kerala topper's "do whatever feels natural" appears in a snippet only).
- Per-question (or at least small-batch) bubbling is better supported than end-of-section bubbling: it is the only routine described first-hand by a top-2 rank.
- With +4 / -1, a guess between two remaining options has positive expected value (+1.5) and a blind four-way guess has +0.25; the "eliminate two, then guess" rule quoted by the topper on MyStudyCart is consistent with that arithmetic. The arithmetic is mine; sources state only the rule.
- A workable hard-section rule assembled from the sources: cap round-one time at about 2 minutes per question, skip on first failure, finish the other sections, and return with the 15 to 20 minute buffer. No topper source describes specifically what they did when a Physics section went badly; this is inferred from general skip rules.
- "Toppers say read summaries, boxes, captions, exercises, Exemplar and the lab manual" is INFERRED folklore as far as this run could establish: coaching pages assert it, but no topper interview or thread opened here says it, and no opened source traces a real question to a summary, box, caption, footnote, exercise, Exemplar item or lab manual.
